# Root Cause Analysis

## 1. Purpose

Root Cause Analysis (RCA) is used to identify the underlying factors that may contribute to observed business performance gaps.

The objective is not to assume causes, but to investigate possible drivers using available data and business evidence.

---

## 2. Root Cause Analysis Approach

The following techniques will be used:

- 5 Whys Analysis
- Fishbone / Ishikawa Analysis
- Data-driven investigation
- KPI comparison
- Segment-level analysis

---

# 3. Issue 1 — Profitability Gap

## Problem Statement

Some products or categories may generate strong revenue without generating proportionally strong profitability.

## Why This Matters

Revenue alone does not indicate whether the business is generating sufficient profit.

## 5 Whys

### Why 1
Why might high-revenue products generate relatively lower profit?

**Possible reason:** Higher discounts, costs, returns, or other business factors may reduce profitability.

### Why 2
Why might these factors reduce profitability?

**Investigation required:** Analyze the relationship between discounts, product-level profit, returns, and other available cost-related variables.

### Why 3
Why are these relationships important?

Because management needs to understand which factors are associated with lower profit margins.

### Why 4
Why does management need this information?

To identify products or categories that require pricing, discount, product-mix, or operational review.

### Why 5
Why should these areas be prioritized?

Because improving profitability requires focusing on the factors that have the greatest measurable business impact.

## Evidence Required

- Revenue by product
- Profit by product
- Profit margin by product
- Discount percentage
- Return rate
- Shipping-related metrics
- Category-level profitability

## Current Conclusion

The available analysis identifies profitability as an area requiring further investigation. A specific root cause should not be concluded until the relevant variables are analyzed together.

# 4. Issue 2 — Higher Fashion Return Rate

## Problem Statement

The Fashion category has a return rate of approximately 8.28%, compared with an overall return rate of approximately 5.52%.

## Why This Matters

A higher return rate may affect customer experience, operational workload, and business profitability.

## 5 Whys

### Why 1
Why is the Fashion return rate higher than the overall return rate?

**Unknown — requires further investigation.**

### Why 2
Are specific Fashion products responsible for a large proportion of returns?

**Investigation required.**

### Why 3
Are returns concentrated among particular customer groups?

**Investigation required.**

### Why 4
Are there common characteristics among returned products or orders?

**Investigation required.**

### Why 5
Is there evidence of a specific underlying cause?

**To be determined through further data analysis.**

## Potential Investigation Areas

- Product-level return rates
- Customer segment
- Age group
- Product characteristics
- Order value
- Discount level
- Repeat return behavior

## Current Conclusion

The Fashion category shows a measurable return-rate gap. However, the available evidence does not establish a specific root cause. Further analysis is required.

# 5. Issue 3 — Revenue Concentration

## Problem Statement

Revenue is concentrated among particular customer groups, products, categories, and regions.

## Current Evidence

The 25–34 age group is currently the largest revenue-contributing age group.

The South region contributes the highest revenue among the analyzed regions.

Electronics is one of the strongest contributors to revenue and unit volume.

## 5 Whys

### Why 1
Why do certain customer groups contribute more revenue?

**Investigation required:** Analyze order frequency, product preferences, and average order value.

### Why 2
Why do certain products/categories contribute more revenue?

**Investigation required:** Analyze sales volume, pricing, customer demand, and product mix.

### Why 3
Why does regional revenue differ?

**Investigation required:** Analyze customer distribution, product preferences, and order behavior by region.

### Why 4
Why is understanding concentration important?

Because changes in high-contributing segments may have a significant effect on overall business performance.

### Why 5
Why should management monitor these segments?

To understand both growth opportunities and potential concentration risks.

## Current Conclusion

Revenue concentration is observable in the current dataset. Further analysis is required to determine the underlying drivers and their profitability implications.

---

# 8. RCA Principle

Root causes should only be identified when supported by evidence.

Observed correlation should not automatically be treated as causation.

Further analysis should therefore be performed before converting potential causes into business requirements or recommendations.
